# Half Lens — Reference Analysis

## Fuel — primary digital benchmark

### Why it is memorable

- The work leads; large media appears before long explanations.
- Macro imagery and oversized type are balanced by tiny factual numbers, dates, and labels.
- A strict grid allows intentional asymmetry without looking arbitrary.
- Native-feeling scrolling, sticky compositions, restrained transform/opacity motion, and light idle animation make the site feel controlled rather than delayed.
- Project pages feel curated, not generated as a uniform card grid.

### Translate for Half Lens

- Use real films and campaign frames as the visual engine.
- Replace Fuel marks and color with Half Lens 45-degree cuts, navy/ice fields, controlled mint, Arabic-first typography, and factual production credits.
- Use chapter numbering and corner metadata only when it helps orientation.
- Let a project frame expand or complete across the split rather than copying Fuel's exact image/type placement.

### Do not copy

Its orange/teal palette, exact hero, `FUEL X` treatment, portraits, navigation placements, crosshair/timeline marks, pricing, testimonials, statistics, contact composition, or pixel-level motion.

## NOB — regional project storytelling

### Useful principles

- Saudi work and client identity are immediately visible.
- Large 16:9 media, strong Arabic project naming, and client marks create credibility without extra claims.
- Project stories use media sequences instead of a generic agency grid.

### Half Lens translation

- Keep the existing one-project-at-a-time editorial rhythm, add a lightweight real archive, and let detail pages support film, production role, client, category, and year.
- Maintain Half Lens navy/ice geometry and avoid NOB's specific typography, card design, or asset density.

## Trend — Arabic hierarchy and long-page rhythm

### Useful principles

- Confident Arabic display typography and clear chapter changes.
- Strong service language and a deliberate closing CTA.
- Regional visual familiarity without relying entirely on English labels.

### Half Lens translation

- Use concise Arabic production language and clearer section entrances.
- Keep Half Lens services cinematic rather than turning them into a conventional agency services list.

### Do not copy

Trend's WordPress/Oxygen stack, repeated sliders, bright green identity, media controls, or dense navigation. Its heavier third-party stack is not a performance model.

## Combined direction

Adopt Fuel's confidence and grid discipline, NOB's project credibility, and Trend's Arabic clarity. Rebuild all three through Half Lens's official color, Cairo typography, 45-degree cut, real work, and existing cinematic scroll architecture.
